SkyWest catches 99.7% of fleet issues after they happen.

An analysis of 6,361 FAA Service Difficulty Records filed against SkyWest Airlines between January 2024 and March 2026 surfaces a single dominant pattern: maintenance posture is overwhelmingly reactive, and risk is concentrated in the oldest airframes operating the highest-utilization regional routes.

§ 01 · The Headline

Three findings worth acting on

analyst's brief
Finding 01 · Fleet Risk
9.97%
The CRJ-200 fleet runs critical at 2.4× the company average.

With an average 44,027 flight hours per airframe — the oldest in the network — the CRJ-200 generated 163 critical events across 104 aircraft. That's 1.57 critical events per tail, the highest in the fleet. Accelerated retirement planning is a defensible recommendation.

Finding 02 · Detection Posture
19 / 6,361
Proactive inspections caught fewer than 0.3% of findings.

Pilot reports (57.5%) and operational events (39.5%) dominate discovery. The maintenance program is finding problems after they affect operations — not before. This is the single largest opportunity for predictive-maintenance ROI in the dataset.

Finding 03 · System Signal
51.5%
"System Warning/Fault" events are critical more often than not.

Of 344 system warnings logged, 177 escalated to critical severity. Landing Gear Position/Warning alone runs 59.4% critical. These signatures are the highest-yield candidates for a predictive alerting model.

§ 06 · What To Do

Four defensible recommendations

analyst's suggestions
Recommendation 01
Build a System-Warning predictive flag.

"System Warning/Fault" is critical 51.5% of the time. Train a lightweight model on these events plus the top three critical-rate systems (Landing Gear Position, Cooling, LG Retraction) to issue priority maintenance tickets. Expected lift: shift even 5% of reactive critical events into proactive catches.

Recommendation 02
Front-load CRJ-200 inspection cycles.

At 9.97% critical rate and 44k+ hours average, the CRJ-200 fleet generates disproportionate risk. Compressing inspection intervals on this fleet only — rather than fleet-wide — protects ops without inflating maintenance hours across the network.

Recommendation 03
Treat the 2025 → 2026 rate trajectory as the headline.

Critical-event rate rose from 3.89% (2024) to 7.10% (2025) to 15.65% (2026 YTD). Even adjusting for reporting lag, this is the metric leadership should track monthly. A single critical-rate KPI is more actionable than raw incident counts.

Recommendation 04
Close the proactive-discovery gap.

Of 19 proactive finds in 27 months, 17 came from Special Inspections and 2 from Bench/Shop. Expanding bench-shop tear-down review to high-cycle airframes — and pairing it with the System-Warning model in Rec 01 — directly attacks the 99.7% reactive ceiling.

§ 07 · Methodology

How this was built

transparent by design
Source
FAA Service Difficulty Reporting System
Federally mandated maintenance findings filed by Part 121 carriers. Operator filter: SkyWest Airlines.
Date Range
Jan 2024 – Mar 2026
27 months. Pre-2024 excluded to avoid post-COVID normalization distortion.
Pipeline
Power Automate · Power Query · Python · Claude AI
Power Automate fetches FAA SDR files on a schedule to OneDrive. Python cleans, deduplicates, and assigns risk scores. Power Query stacks monthly files. Claude AI generated the dashboard front-end.
Risk Scoring
5-tier severity model
Score 5 = Critical (system failure, fracture, fail-language). Score 1 = Routine finding. Rules documented and reproducible.
